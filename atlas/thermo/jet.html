Jet Engine Lab

A business-jet-class turbofan running the Brayton cycle, and its air-conditioning pack running the reverse Brayton cycle. Fly it, make parts transparent, isolate any component as a control volume, and watch the working fluid change state. Every number comes from the Chapter 4 energy balances with the course's cold/hot ideal-gas model, so you can reproduce it by hand.

Model notes

Generic two-spool high-bypass turbofan (bypass ratio 4.5, overall pressure ratio ≈ 36, T₄,max = 1650 K, 230 kg/s at sea-level takeoff), representative of the 60–80 kN engines on large business jets. Cold section c_p = 1.005 kJ/kg·K, k = 1.4; hot section c_p = 1.148, k = 1.333; LHV = 43 MJ/kg. Component isentropic efficiencies: fan 90 %, LPC 88 %, HPC 87 %, HPT 91 %, LPT 92 %; combustor 99.5 % with 4 % pressure loss; nozzles fully expanded with velocity coefficient 0.98. Off-design behaviour (idle, altitude) is a simple spool-speed scaling, not a full component-matching model. Station numbering follows the usual engine convention (0 ambient, 2 fan face, 13 bypass, 2.5, 3, 4, 4.5, 5, 8).
