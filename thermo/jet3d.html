Jet 3D: X-ray the Aircraft

A generic twin-engine jet you can rotate, see through and take apart. The two engines run the Brayton cycle; the bleed-air duct carries compressor air forward to the environmental-control pack behind the pilot's seat, where the air cycle machine runs the reverse Brayton cycle to cool it for the cockpit. Three things that look alike (and aren't)

The compressor is a fan that uses work to push air up in pressure. The turbine is the same idea run backwards: a fan that makes work as gas falls in pressure. The nozzle is a funnel that turns whatever pressure is left into speed.

So the hot gas expands twice: first through the turbine, where the expansion is harvested as shaft work (3 → 4 in class notation, exactly the work the compressor, and on a turboprop the propeller, needs), then through the nozzle, where the expansion is harvested as jet velocity (4 → 5, V₅ = √2(h₄ − h₅)). No shaft, no work, just h → kinetic energy.

Try it: blade rows that get smaller as the air is squeezed; blade rows and the shaft running forward to the compressor; just the converging–diverging cone, no shaft at all. In the pack the same trio appears in miniature: and on one shaft.
