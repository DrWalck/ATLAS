Key ideas: second-law statements, Carnot limits and the Clausius inequality

Two classical statements

Clausius: it is impossible for any system to operate so that the sole result is heat transfer from a cooler to a hotter body. Kelvin–Planck: it is impossible for any system to run a cycle and deliver net work while receiving heat from a single thermal reservoir. Analytical form:

Irreversibilities (what makes a real process fall short)

Heat transfer through a finite ΔT · unrestrained expansion to a lower pressure · spontaneous chemical reaction · spontaneous mixing · friction (sliding and fluid) · electric current through a resistance · inelastic deformation. A process is internally reversible when none of these are present inside the system (a quasi-equilibrium process); irreversibilities may still exist in the surroundings.

Carnot corollaries

1. An irreversible power cycle always has a lower thermal efficiency than a reversible one operating between the same two reservoirs ("real is always less than ideal"). 2. All reversible power cycles between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency — an ideal Rankine plant and an ideal Brayton plant between the same T_H and T_C tie. The same two statements hold for the COP of refrigerators and heat pumps.

Kelvin temperature scale

For a reversible cycle, and only then, the ratio of the heat transfers equals the ratio of the absolute reservoir temperatures. This is what lets temperature replace heat in every limit below.

Maximum performance between two reservoirs

Eqs. 5.9–5.11. Actual: η = W_cycle/Q_H = 1 − Q_C/Q_H, β = Q_C/W_cycle, γ = Q_H/W_cycle. Compare actual to maximum: actual < max → irreversible, = max → reversible, > max → impossible.

Clausius inequality

Eqs. 5.13–5.14. Evaluate δQ/T at the boundary temperature, over the whole cycle; the bodies supplying or receiving heat need not be reservoirs. For a two-reservoir cycle: Q_H/T_H − Q_C/T_C = −σ_cycle. σ_cycle is the entropy produced by irreversibilities inside the system — the starting point of Chapter 6.
